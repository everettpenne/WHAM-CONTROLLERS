# 1. Project overview

Snapshot: 2026-11-20 (repository map updated for the reorganization on branch `reorg`); the rest as of 2026-11-16, `main` after `666fd35`.

## 1.1 What this project is

One controller board, the **PFM-Controller V4** (an STM32G474 microcontroller plus fiber-optic and electrical I/O), runs one of
two firmware products. Each product turns serial commands into precisely timed switching signals for a power supply and watches
that supply's fault signals.

| Product | Firmware folder | Name in `*IDN?` | Drives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XREX** | `firmware/xrex/` | `WHAM-XREX-PFMG474` | Up to **four Transrex ISR-2126 magnet supplies**, one independent channel each. Each channel sends a PFM "demand" frequency to its supply and reads back a PFM "unit current" frequency. A PID loop closes between them. | v0.8i. Closed loop proven on bench loopbacks and against a simulator board. **Not yet commissioned on a real Transrex**: the current calibration is placeholder. |
| **HBRIDGE** | `firmware/hbridge/` | `WHAM-HBRIDGE-PFMG474` | Three output phases U, V and W, each with a complementary output and hardware dead time. Two supply types, chosen at runtime: the **Limiter** (PWM, fixed carrier, duty varies), which is single-phase per electrode: each phase (for example U/UN) drives one independently biased electrode with its own duty waveform (one electrode today, up to three); and the **HVPS** (PFM, frequency varies, about 50 % duty), a three-phase H-bridge. Reads 12 gate-driver status (GDS) fault inputs. | v0.11. Open-loop shots verified on hardware with a logic analyzer. Closed loop (ADC voltage feedback) tested against firmware plant models and loopbacks, **not on a real supply**. One unit is installed in the ECH gyrotron HVPS. |

"PFM" is pulse-frequency modulation: the information is the switching frequency. "PWM" is pulse-width modulation: the
information is the duty cycle. Both are generated by the microcontroller's **HRTIM** (high-resolution timer) peripheral.

Serial numbers have the form `WB-NNNN-C`, where C is a Luhn check digit. They are stored in the board's external flash and
reported by `*IDN?` and `SYS:ID?`. The registry is `host/devices.csv`. The network addresses, MACs and hub ports are in
`docs/bench_devices.md`.

## 1.3 How you reach a board

```
your computer --ssh tunnel--> lab server "andrew" --> wham_hub.py (one per board) --> WIZnet serial-to-Ethernet bridge --> board UART (115200 8N1)
```

* Each board's serial port is exposed on the lab network by a **WIZnet serial-to-Ethernet module** that accepts **only one TCP
  client**. That client is the **hub** (`host/wham_hub.py`), which shares the board with any number of users, logs every line,
  and announces dangerous commands to the other users.
* On the bench a board can also be reached directly by a USB-serial cable (CH340 chip) and programmed with an **ST-Link** (SWD).
* Commands are text lines in an SCPI-like style (`*IDN?`, `STATE?`, `SHOT:STEP 0 20000 50`). Every reply is one line starting
  with `OK` or `ERR <code>`.

## 1.4 Requirements, as they stand

These are the requirements that the code currently meets or is built around. Numbers in `ctrlr_config.h` are the source of truth.

**Both products**
* Commands over a serial link at 115200 baud, usable through the network bridge, with strict argument checking (a malformed
  number is refused, never guessed).
* An operating-state machine: `IDLE -> ARM -> ARMED -> FIRE (or trigger) -> FIRING -> IDLE`, and `FAULT` from any state. A shot
  needs a fresh `ARM` each time. Faults latch until `FAULT:CLEar`, which re-checks the fault inputs before leaving `FAULT`.
* In-application firmware update over the serial link (dual flash bank, CRC check, swap, rollback), so boards can be updated
  without physical access.
* Saved settings profiles in the external flash (`*SAV`, `*RCL`, 8 slots), loaded only on request, never at boot.
* Board identity (MCU unique ID, flash unique ID, provisioned serial number) reported on every connection.
* Front-panel LEDs for serial activity and operating state.

**XREX**
* Four independent channels. No frequency relationship between them.
* A fixed-rate PID compute step (default 1 kHz, `CONFig:PIDRate`), independent of each channel's own carrier frequency.
* Output clamped to 3,000-150,000 Hz. The 3 kHz floor is a hardware limit of the HRTIM 16-bit period register.
* Shots defined as ramp, flat top and ramp down to a demand current per channel; amps converted to hertz by a calibration
  (currently a **linear placeholder**).
* Per-channel faults (water flow, temperature, Enerpro firing board, overcurrent) and per-channel enable/contactor outputs to the
  Transrex. A system-wide external-enable interlock (PF13) and an external trigger (PF15).
* On a fault while firing: the faulted channel stops at once, and the others ramp down rather than cut off. On overcurrent, the
  survivors first step down by `100/N` percent.

**HBRIDGE**
* Three output phases (HVPS: the three phases of one bridge; Limiter: one phase per electrode), U at 0°, V at 120° and W at 240° of each period (needed by the HVPS; for the Limiter only a carry-over, no particular phase relationship is required), with complementary outputs and dead time (default 100 ns,
  settable 50-3000 ns with `CONFig:DEADtime`).
* Supply mode limits, checked when a shot is uploaded: Limiter 75-125 kHz and 5-95 % duty; HVPS 10-30 kHz and 40-60 % duty.
* Open-loop shots: up to 500 time-stamped setpoints (time, frequency, duty, optionally a duty per phase), up to 1 s long,
  interpolated linearly one switching period at a time. A 0 Hz setpoint is an output-off gap.
* Closed loop: output voltage held to a time-series target using ADC feedback, one loop per phase (Limiter), or one frequency loop
  (HVPS), with plant-model feedforward.
* Any of the 12 GDS inputs in its fault state stops the output at once (no ramp). Inputs can be switched off or relabeled, and
  their polarity flipped, at runtime.
* A rising edge on PF15 while armed starts the shot (handled by interrupt).
* A separate 50 % square wave on PG0 (`CHARGE:FREQuency`) for the capacitor-bank charger, independent of faults.
